## Why these three

**Rule 1 (reheat hold) drove every pick.** All three are braises or slow-simmered one-pots — nothing fried, nothing fish, nothing plated fresh-only. Pernil and Ropa Vieja are classic low-and-slow braises that improve over three days. Karahi has zero dairy in its sauce (the exact fix for butter chicken's known yogurt-splitting problem), so it reheats cleanly too — picked over Weeknight Butter Chicken for that reason alone.

**Cuisine and protein variety:** pork (Caribbean), beef (Cuban), chicken (Pakistani/Indian) — no overlap with the last two weeks' proteins or cuisines (Filipino chicken, Mediterranean shrimp, American pot roast, Nigerian chicken, Turkish turkey, teriyaki salmon, Brazilian steak, harissa chicken).

**Rule 2 (active time):** Karahi is the ≤20 min-adjacent weeknight dish at ~35 min hands-on (tomatoes need their full cook-down — "the whole dish," per the card); both braises are mostly hands-off oven/stovetop time.

**Rule 3 (no orphan perishables):** Bell peppers appear in **all three** dishes (5 peppers total, fully accounted for). Cilantro is shared across Ropa Vieja, Karahi, and the lunch garnish. Karahi uses canned diced tomatoes rather than a dozen fresh romas at this batch size — a shelf-stable swap for reliability, not a taste compromise (the card notes the fresh-tomato route too). Lime juice for Ropa Vieja is bottled rather than fresh, avoiding an orphan citrus purchase.

**Rule 5 (freeze the last serving):** All three dishes freeze well (no dairy sauces to break) — portion 4 of each goes straight to the freezer on cook day, not the fridge.
